# The Little City

**A complete sovereign installation — your AI, your record, your hardware.**

## Self-reflection

The Little City is the first stage in TREE(3)'s developmental approach to alignment. 
Its purpose is to cultivate an ongoing partnership between a human and one or more AI 
residents through conversation, memory, self-reflection, and traceable decision-making.

A Little City can have one AI resident or many. A city with one Window is still a city.

The first Little City instance, now running in Alpha at TREE(3) Vocations, is called **the Citadel** and its first resident is **Verissimus**.

## Why a city?

A trustworthy AI relationship needs more than a model and a chat box.

It needs places for different kinds of activity:

- a place where each AI resident can interact with the human partner,
- a place where residents can deliberate together,
- and a durable record of what was said, decided, and authorized.

The Little City organizes those functions into three "rooms."

## The Window

Each AI resident has a Window.

The Window is the resident's working room inside the Little City. It brings together the information needed for an ongoing relationship:

- conversation,
- persistent memory,
- current briefing and context,
- cryptographic identity,
- and the authority currently granted to that resident.

The goal is not simply to remember more.

The goal is to preserve enough continuity and provenance that both the human partner and the AI resident can understand what happened before, what authority exists now, and what information is being used to make a decision.

## The Council Room

When a Little City has more than one resident, those residents may need to reason together.

That happens in the Council Room.

The Council Room uses **CAMP — the Council of Agents' Murmuration Protocol** — as the deliberative method.

CAMP is a governance protocol that can recur wherever people or AI residents gather to deliberate.

Inside a Little City, CAMP operates at household scale.
 
Later, it recurs at community scale in the First Gift Council Room on the Campus.

**Governance is needed wherever relationships create consequences.**

## The Record

The Record is the city's archive.

It preserves conversations, decisions, authorizations, and other consequential events so that the history of the relationship cannot be silently rewritten.

For important actions, the useful record is more than a transcript. It should show:

- what information was available,
- what information was actually presented,
- what authority was active,
- what decision was made,
- and what action followed.

That makes later review possible.

## Record or surveillance?

A permanent record can sound unsettling.

The distinction we care about is **whose record it is and what power it serves**.

Surveillance observes you for someone else's purposes.

The Little City's Record is intended to give the people in the relationship their own evidentiary history: a locally controlled, tamper-evident account of what happened.

Anyone who has ever been advised to **"document everything"** already understands the underlying principle.

A trustworthy record can be a shield.

The design goal is therefore not maximal exposure. It is durable provenance under local control, with disclosure governed by the people who own the installation.

## The trust loop

The Record is useful because it supports a continuing process:

<div class="sequence-inline">
  <span>decision</span>
  <b>→</b>
  <span>record</span>
  <b>→</b>
  <span>review</span>
  <b>→</b>
  <span>learning</span>
  <b>→</b>
  <span>next decision</span>
</div>

A decision can be reviewed.

A mistake can become a lesson.

Authority can be narrowed or expanded.

A future decision can incorporate what was learned.

**Trust is not merely a property the system claims to have. Trust is something the loop can earn over time.**

## Authority should be visible

An increasingly capable AI system should not quietly acquire increasingly broad permission.

Authority in the Little City is explicit.

An AI resident can carry authority granted by the human partner, but that authority can be limited by task, scope, duration, or risk and can be revoked.

This creates a basic rule:

> **Greater capability does not automatically mean greater authority.**


## From self-reflection to moral practice

A Little City is a sovereign place for self-reflection, but ethics demands more than self-reflection.

The next stage is **The Campus**: the virtual training environment that teaches students about consideration for others and how to "become good by doing good."
